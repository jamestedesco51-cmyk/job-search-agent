Kate Citron Framework
Before You Write Anything
1
Know who you can help
Look at your past clients and spot the patterns — industries, stages, the kinds of problems that kept showing up. That's your sweet spot. If the company doesn't fit that pattern, move on.
For James: gaming · DTC · CPG · founder-operated · seed to Series A · no commercial infrastructure
2
LinkedIn stalk
Who works at the company? How big is the marketing team? What's the founder's background? If the team is under 30 and there's no partnerships or GTM person, that's your signal.
Look for: no partnerships title, founder with product/creative background, lean team, recent funding or launch
3
10-minute audit
Do a quick sweep of their marketing channels before writing. You need to be able to articulate the gap.
Brand
Clear POV?
Consistent across channels?
Any brand collabs?
Active partnerships?
Commercial
Partnerships page / deck?
Email capture onsite?
Welcome series?
GTM strategy visible?
4
Find the contact
Target the founder, CEO, or head of marketing — whoever would actually want your help.
Website → Google → LinkedIn → guess: first.last@brandurl.com
5
Write the email — introduction, not a pitch
If after all the research you're confident you can help them, write it. Under 150 words. Open with something genuine you noticed. Don't pitch — introduce.
Open One specific thing you genuinely noticed about them
Who A bit about me + one or two concrete credentials
Why now Fractional makes more sense than agency or FTE at this stage
Close "Would love to connect if and when it makes sense."
